THE CORE IDEA

A workflow is more than a list of tools.

A useful workflow explains why each step exists, what evidence it produces, and what you can — and cannot — conclude from it.

01

Start with a biological question

Define the problem before opening a database or software. The same tool can support very different questions.

02

Match evidence to the claim

A database annotation, computational prediction, structural observation, and experimental finding should not be treated as interchangeable evidence.

03

Finish with an interpretable output

The goal is not simply to complete steps. Produce a result that can be understood, evaluated, and built on.

EVIDENCE MODEL

Not every result supports the same kind of conclusion.

GeneBite keeps evidence type visible so that observations, annotations, predictions, and experimental findings are not accidentally treated as equivalent.

DATABASE / CURATED RECORD

What has been recorded?

Useful for identifiers, curated annotations, sequence records, structural records, and database-linked information.

COMPUTATIONAL EVIDENCE

What does the analysis suggest?

Includes sequence similarity, structural prediction, statistical analysis, and model-based inference.

EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE

What was observed under defined conditions?

Experimental findings are observations under defined conditions, with their own controls, measurements, uncertainty, and limitations.

SCIENTIFIC QUALITY

A workflow should make uncertainty visible.

Scientific reasoning becomes stronger when assumptions, evidence type, uncertainty, and limitations are explicit.

OBSERVATION

What did the data actually show?

Describe the result before explaining what you think it means.

INFERENCE

What does the evidence suggest?

Keep interpretation proportional to the strength and type of evidence.

LIMITATION

What remains uncertain?

A strong workflow does not hide what the current evidence cannot establish.
