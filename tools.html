HOW TO CHOOSE A TOOL

Choose by input, purpose, and expected output.

Before opening a tool, make the research task explicit.

01

What do I have?

A sequence, protein ID, structure, compound, paper, dataset, or experimental measurements?

02

What do I want to know?

Identity, function, similarity, structure, activity, relationships, statistics, or literature evidence?

03

What will the output support?

Decide whether the result is a database record, experimental observation, computational prediction, analytical result, or visualization.

RESEARCH TOOLCHAINS

COMMON TOOL MISTAKES

A tool can give you a result without giving you the right conclusion.

01

Using a tool without a question

Opening a database or software first can create a large amount of information without a clear interpretation path.

02

Ignoring input quality

Incorrect identifiers, incomplete sequences, poor data structure, or missing experimental context can produce misleading outputs.

03

Confusing prediction with experiment

Computational predictions can generate useful hypotheses, but they should not automatically be presented as experimental observations.

04

Ignoring tool limitations

Every database, algorithm, model, and visualization has assumptions and limitations that affect interpretation.
