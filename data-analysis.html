CORE IDEA

The statistical test comes after the research question.

The number of groups is only one part of the decision. Think about the outcome, predictor, dependence between observations, study design and assumptions before choosing a test.

Q
Research question What biological relationship, difference or prediction are you trying to investigate?
D
Data structure Identify variables, groups, repeated measurements, paired observations and missing data.
A
Assumptions Check whether the selected model or test is appropriate for your data and design.

TEST SELECTION

Choose the analysis from the design, not just the group count.

The following are common starting points. Real analyses may require additional checks or more appropriate models.

TWO GROUPS

t-test

A common framework for comparing means of two groups, with the exact form depending on whether observations are independent or paired and on assumptions.

Compare
MULTIPLE GROUPS

ANOVA

Used to model differences in means across multiple groups. Significant omnibus results do not by themselves identify which groups differ.

Compare
RELATIONSHIP

Correlation

Quantifies association between variables. Association does not by itself establish causation.

Relationship
PREDICTION

Regression

Models an outcome as a function of one or more predictors and can estimate associations while accounting for included variables.

Model
NON-PARAMETRIC

Rank-based methods

Methods such as Mann–Whitney or Wilcoxon can be useful for particular designs and data structures; they are not simply automatic replacements for parametric tests.

Alternative
MULTIVARIATE

Multivariate analysis

When several variables are analyzed together, methods such as PCA or multivariable models may help reveal structure that single-variable tests miss.

DATA BASICS

Before statistics, understand what each row actually represents.

The experimental unit matters. Technical measurements are not automatically independent biological replicates.

01
Biological replicates Independent biological units that provide information about biological variability.
02
Technical replicates Repeated measurements of the same biological unit. They help characterize measurement variability but are not automatically independent samples.
03
Repeated measurements Measurements from the same experimental unit over time or conditions require the dependence structure to be considered.
RELATIONSHIPS

When the question is about relationships between variables.

Choose the method based on the scientific question, variable types, data structure and assumptions.

ASSOCIATION

Correlation

Useful when the focus is the strength and direction of association between variables. The interpretation depends on the correlation measure and data characteristics.

MODELING

Regression

Useful when an outcome is modeled using one or more predictors. Model assumptions, confounding and the study design remain important.

CAUSALITY

Association ≠ causation

A statistical association does not by itself establish that changing one variable causes a change in another.

MODELING

A model is a structured way to ask a quantitative question.

Regression and related models can incorporate multiple predictors, but adding variables does not automatically make a model scientifically better.

Y
Define the outcome What biological quantity are you trying to explain or predict?
X
Define predictors Which variables are scientifically relevant to the question?
C
Consider confounding A third variable can distort or explain an observed association.
MULTIVARIATE

When one variable is not enough to describe the biology.

High-dimensional biological datasets often require methods that consider many variables together.

PCA

Principal Component Analysis

PCA can summarize major patterns of variation in multivariable data by transforming correlated variables into principal components.

OMICS

High-dimensional datasets

Transcriptomics, proteomics and other omics datasets may contain many variables relative to the number of biological samples.

INTERPRETATION

Pattern ≠ mechanism

A multivariate pattern can generate a biological hypothesis, but additional evidence may be needed to establish its mechanism.

INTERPRETATION

A statistically significant result is not the whole story.

Interpret statistical evidence together with effect size, uncertainty, biological context and study design.

p
p-value A quantity defined relative to a null model and test procedure. It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true.
CI
Confidence interval Provides an interval estimate under the specified statistical procedure and helps communicate uncertainty around an estimate.
Δ
Effect size Describes the magnitude of an observed difference or association and helps move beyond a binary significant/not-significant interpretation.
VISUALIZATION

Plot the data before you tell the story.

Good visualization can reveal distributions, outliers, variability and patterns that summary statistics alone may hide.

DISTRIBUTION

Show the data

Use plots that allow readers to see individual observations when appropriate, rather than relying only on bars and summary values.

VARIABILITY

Show uncertainty

Error bars need clear definitions. State whether they represent SD, SE, confidence intervals or another quantity.

CONTEXT

Keep biological context

A visually striking pattern is not automatically biologically meaningful.

SCIENTIFIC QUALITY

Make the analysis reproducible and defensible.

01
Predefine the question Know what comparison, association or prediction you are testing before exploring the result.
02
Document the analysis Record software, version, preprocessing, statistical method, parameters and relevant decisions.
03
Report limitations Mention sample size, missing data, assumptions, multiple testing and other relevant limitations.
04
Separate result from interpretation State what the analysis showed before explaining what you think it means biologically.
