CORE IDEA

Do not start with the picture. Start with the protein.

A 3D structure becomes useful only when you know what protein it represents, how it was obtained, and what biological question you are asking.

IDENTITY

Are you analyzing the correct protein?

Check accession, organism, isoform, sequence and relevant biological context before choosing a structure.

STRUCTURE

What kind of structure are you looking at?

Experimental structures and computational predictions are different evidence types and should be reported separately.

INTERPRETATION

What does the structure actually tell you?

A visual feature becomes meaningful only when connected to sequence, known biology, ligands, interfaces or other evidence.

THE WORKFLOW

Five steps from protein identity to interpretation.

The structure is one layer of evidence, not the whole biological story.

01 · IDENTIFY Database evidence

Identify the correct protein

Start with a stable identifier rather than relying only on a common protein name.

Check

  • UniProt accession
  • Organism
  • Protein length
  • Canonical sequence / isoform
UniProt · NCBI
02 · EXPLORE Sequence context

Explore sequence and functional annotations

Understand the sequence before moving into 3D structure.

Look for

  • Protein domains
  • Functional sites
  • Subcellular localization
  • Known variants or isoforms
UniProt · InterPro
03 · STRUCTURE Structural evidence

Find an appropriate 3D structure

A PDB entry is not automatically the correct structure for your question. Compare the entry with the protein you actually want to study.

Compare

  • Sequence / construct
  • Organism
  • Chain and residue coverage
  • Experimental method and resolution
  • Ligands or bound molecules
RCSB PDB
04 · INSPECT Visual analysis

Inspect the structure with molecular visualization

Visualization is useful when it answers a question. Do not change colors or representations without a reason.

Useful actions

  • Highlight domains or chains
  • Inspect conserved residues
  • Locate ligands
  • Inspect pockets and interfaces
VMD · PyMOL
05 · INTERPRET Biological reasoning

Turn structural observations into biological questions

A structure becomes scientifically useful when you connect what you see to sequence, literature and biological context.

Ask

  • What is experimentally observed?
  • Which features are predicted?
  • Does the structure support known biology?
  • What should be tested next?

What should you inspect?

Useful observations

  • Overall fold
  • β-barrel architecture
  • Chromophore location
  • Residues surrounding the chromophore
INTERPRETATION

Connect the chromophore to the structure

کروموفور GFP از Ser65، Tyr66 و Gly67 ساخته می‌شود. در viewer می‌توانی این residueها و محیط سه‌بعدی‌شان را ببینی.

Important distinction

Seeing a residue near a chromophore does not by itself prove that the residue determines fluorescence. Structural observation is the starting point for a biological question.

STRUCTURE EVIDENCE

Before interpreting a structure, record where it came from.

EXPERIMENTAL

Experimental structure

Determine the experimental method, resolution and construct. These details affect what can reasonably be interpreted from the structure.

PREDICTED

Computational structure

For predicted structures, report the prediction source and consider confidence measures rather than presenting the model as experimental observation.

BIOLOGICAL CONTEXT

Structure is not the whole answer

Connect structural observations to sequence, biochemical data, literature, ligand information and the biological system.

COMMON MISTAKES

What can make structural analysis misleading?

01

Choosing a PDB entry by name alone

Check sequence, construct, organism, chain coverage, method and resolution before using an entry.

02

Confusing a visualization with an analysis

Changing colors or representations is not automatically a biological result. Every visualization should answer a question.

03

Treating structural proximity as proof

Two residues being close in 3D space does not by itself establish a functional relationship.

04

Ignoring missing or engineered regions

Experimental constructs may contain mutations, truncations, tags or unresolved residues that differ from the native protein.

05

Ignoring the evidence source

Always distinguish experimental measurements from computational predictions.

06

Stopping at structural description

The research value comes from connecting the structure to a biological question, evidence and a next step.
