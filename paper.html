SCIENTIFIC PAPER READING

Read a scientific paper like a researcher, not from page one to the last page.

A practical workflow for extracting the research question, experimental logic, evidence, figures, limitations and actionable takeaways from a scientific paper.

CORE PRINCIPLE

Don't read every sentence with equal attention.

Researcher's rule:

Separate what the authors observed, what they inferred and what you can reasonably conclude from the evidence.

STEP-BY-STEP

Turn a paper into a structured research understanding.

Move through the paper by asking research questions rather than simply following its page order.

01
RESEARCH QUESTION

What problem is the paper trying to address?

Before diving into technical details, identify the central research question and the gap the study is trying to address.

Find

  • Research problem
  • Knowledge gap
  • Main hypothesis
  • Primary objective

Useful question

If I had to explain this paper in one sentence without using its title, what would I say?

Research question
02
ABSTRACT

Use the abstract as a map, not as the whole paper.

The abstract gives you the study's compressed logic. Use it to build expectations before reading the detailed evidence.

Extract
  • Question
  • Approach
  • Main finding
  • Main conclusion
Then ask

Does the rest of the paper provide enough evidence for the main claim presented in the abstract?

03
INTRODUCTION

Understand why this question matters.

The introduction should give you enough biological and scientific context to understand the question, not force you to memorize every citation.

Track the logic

01 What is already known?
02 What remains unclear?
03 Why does the gap matter?
04 What does this study propose to investigate?

Don't lose the question

If a paragraph does not help you understand the biological problem, mechanism, hypothesis or rationale, you may not need to study it with the same depth.

04
METHODS

Can the study design actually address the question?

Methods are where you examine the logic connecting the research question to the evidence.

Model What biological system was studied?
Samples What samples or groups were compared?
Controls What controls support the interpretation?
Replicates How many biological or technical replicates were used?
Measurement What exactly was measured?
Statistics Which statistical analysis was used, and why?
A statistically significant result does not automatically establish a biological claim.

Statistical evidence should be interpreted together with effect size, uncertainty, experimental design, controls and biological context.

Study design
05
FIGURE READING

Read the figure before reading the authors' interpretation.

Figureها بخش مهم شواهدند. اول مشخص کن چه چیزی اندازه‌گیری شده و چه مقایسه‌ای را نشان می‌دهد.

01 What are the groups?
02 What is the control?
03 What is on each axis?
04 What does each point or bar represent?
05 What does n represent?
06 What statistical comparison was made?
07 What can you conclude from this figure alone?
Figure-first rule

Write your own one-sentence interpretation before reading the paragraph that explains the figure.

06
RESULTS

What do the data actually show?

Results should connect observations to the research question without extending beyond what the data support.

01 Observation

What changed?

→
02 Comparison

Compared with what?

→
03 Evidence

What supports the observation?

→
04 Meaning

What biological interpretation is justified?

Observed result
07
DISCUSSION

Where do the authors move from evidence to interpretation?

در Discussion نویسندگان معنای نتایج را می‌گویند. تو تفسیر پشتیبانی‌شده را از حدس و ادعای بدون شواهد جدا کن.

Evidence

Supported interpretation

The interpretation is consistent with the presented data, controls and study design.

Interpretation

Needs context

The interpretation may be plausible but depends on assumptions, previous literature or additional evidence.

08
CRITICAL EVALUATION

What can this paper support — and what remains uncertain?

A strong paper-reading workflow ends by testing the limits of the evidence rather than simply accepting the authors' conclusion.

Design Does the design match the research question?
Controls Are the controls appropriate for the claim?
Sample Is the sample appropriate for the research question?
Statistics Are the statistical methods appropriate for the data and design?
Generalization How far can the findings reasonably be generalized?
Limitations What important limitation remains?
Critical evaluation

COMMON MISTAKES

Reading a paper can go wrong in predictable ways.

01

Reading linearly from page one

You may spend too much time on background before understanding what the study is actually testing.

02

Reading the discussion before the figures

You risk adopting the authors' interpretation before independently examining the evidence.

03

Ignoring controls

Without understanding the control, it can be difficult to know what an observed change actually means.

04

Confusing correlation with causation

An observed association does not automatically establish a causal mechanism.

05

Ignoring sample size and variability

A visually large difference still needs careful interpretation when sample size and variability are considered.

06

Taking the conclusion as established fact

A conclusion is an interpretation of the evidence and should be evaluated against the study design, data and limitations.
